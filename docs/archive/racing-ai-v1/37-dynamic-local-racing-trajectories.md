## Recommendation and scope

Retain one shared track/reference representation and let each car choose a short local trajectory, including a speed schedule. Optimise useful forward racing progress and corner exit; merely returning to the guide sooner has no intrinsic value. A car that runs wide may sensibly rejoin after the apex or on the next straight. A passing car may stay on its alternate path while that is quicker and available.

Implementation is a substantive, bounded AI milestone. Existing world snapshots, equipment, geometry, controller, physical rollouts, worker ownership, telemetry and test fixtures are reusable. Extend candidate geometry and scoring; preserve the playable71 reference. Do not replace the entire game or add a full-track optimisation per car each frame.

The present code already builds private paths: `ContextPathState` holds one smooth lateral transition; `ContextPathFollower` samples41 points over120m; `ContextDriverPolicy` updates selection about every100ms; physical forecasts and following/guard controls check the result. Its limitation is a restricted family of offset transitions and a locally scored destination. This differs from comparing several corner-aware approaches and their exit conditions. Current guide rejoining also blends over a fixed65m look-ahead rather than choosing a useful rejoin point from competing trajectories.

## Proposed planning flow

1. Share static track samples, boundaries, scenery constraints, corner landmarks and guide information across cars. Cache equipment-specific capability data by complete relevant configuration, with invalidation for changes. Keep live car observations separate and immutable during each planning batch.
2. From actual position, velocity, heading, yaw and steering state, construct a bounded set of continuous curves through upcoming track sections. Include continuing the current plan, holding a contested side, a feasible pass, and several future rejoin opportunities. Start with at most six proposals as an experiment budget, not six permanent lanes. Candidate endpoints and tangents are continuous values. Reject geometric impossibilities cheaply before detailed physics.
3. Produce a speed schedule for each curve using its curvature and the car's real acceleration/braking/traction limits. Include sufficient preview to stop for the next restriction and evaluate a meaningful corner exit. A two-to-four-second local tactical horizon is a starting hypothesis; braking distance and corner structure determine required geometric preview. Keep prediction uncertainty explicit rather than pretending another driver's steering is known four seconds ahead.
4. Compare expected time to the same downstream track gate, plus the physically achievable continuation after it. A short path ending at a poor angle or low speed must not win by ignoring its exit. A candidate that crosses the guide earlier gets no automatic advantage. When traffic blocks all passes, ordinary following remains a valid outcome.
5. Fully evaluate only a bounded shortlist with the shared actual vehicle forecast and road/scenery/swept-body traffic checks. Keep current collision response and physical recovery. An unresolved selection must remain explicitly unresolved; do not silently certify an old path. The known71 rear-reservation conflict belongs in this work.
6. Execute the chosen plan through ordinary steering/pedal inputs and re-evaluate on fresh observations. Retain a still-viable plan when alternatives offer negligible benefit; switch promptly for a changed hazard or a meaningful pace gain. Continuity includes heading/curvature and actual motion, not just remembering a side preference.

The first implementation should isolate clear-track rejoining. Avoid introducing traffic prediction, new curve families and personality all at once. A simple spline/lattice candidate generator with a bounded search is a suitable first prototype. An unrestricted nonlinear optimiser per car is unnecessary for that experiment.

## Research basis and limits

TUM's planner separates offline track preparation from online local search, then refines selected routes and generates matching velocity profiles. This supports reusing track work while updating a local plan. Its Python racing-vehicle implementation and modelling assumptions are not a drop-in Unity/game-physics solution. Our bounded candidate counts, cadence, gate-time objective and staged rollout above are project proposals, not claims from the research. [Authors' framework documentation](https://graphbasedlocaltrajectoryplanner.readthedocs.io/en/latest/start/overview.html), [project repository](https://github.com/TUMFTM/GraphBasedLocalTrajectoryPlanner).

Current experiments reinforce the need to evaluate the combination:72's longer cheap passing score caused recovery and a DNF, while73's local speed envelope improves car0 by3.324188s but worsens total field time by25.227173s. Both remain unpromoted. Neither proves a dynamic trajectory planner will meet our pace or CPU targets.
