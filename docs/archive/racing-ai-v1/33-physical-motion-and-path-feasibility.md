# Physical motion and path feasibility

This is the bounded next step after prototype 14 failed the full-field road-envelope check. It supports the racing milestone in [document 32](32-racing-ai-ground-truth-and-implementation.md), not a replacement campaign scope or a new tactical planner.

## Evidence and decision

Car 16's selected offset stayed within the nominal road corridor, but its actual body exceeded the switchback edge by 0.203 m. Two contact incidents also replaced the prior field's single lower-speed incident. The improved collective time does not compensate for these failures. Preserve 12 as the prior passing comparison; 14 remains a reproducible failing experiment. Do not change road width, shared forces, blanket margins or corner-specific offsets to erase the failure.

The immediate question is whether the controller requests a dynamically infeasible approach, whether its follower misses a feasible path, or both. Those diagnoses need different corrections. A geometric target on the road is insufficient evidence of a feasible motion.

## 1. Calibrate the physical model independently of decisions

`PlanarDrivePrediction` models the existing car on flat tarmac: steering response, the shared `DriveModel.Forces`, lateral tyre force, torque servo, angular damping and velocity integration. It does not change a Rigidbody, opponent, equipment value or physical rule. Contacts, road pitch/roll and future driver decisions are outside this initial model.

The implementation follows the actual `RoadCar` force path. Unity documents that `AddTorque` with acceleration mode applies angular acceleration over the physics timestep, independently of mass ([Unity API](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/Rigidbody.AddTorque.html)). The public PhysX integrator shows explicit damping and quaternion integration ([NVIDIA source](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX/blob/main/physx/source/lowleveldynamics/src/DyBodyCoreIntegrator.h)). These sources explain the starting approximation; the installed Unity build's measured Rigidbody behaviour is the acceptance evidence. No external planner or physics code is imported.

`MotionPredictionAudit` starts independent rolling model states every 0.1 s. Each consumes the actual applied input history as it arrives and compares its state against later physics observations at 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 s. This is known-input replay for model calibration, **not a prospective forecast with access to future commands**. The live driver receives none of these predictions.

Keep contact, slope, off-surface and tilted-body windows in the output, labelled separately. The initial calibration domain requires every step grounded, road normal and body-up y above 0.999, centre on tarmac and no new scored collision/recovery. Use actual transformed collider bottom corners for observed road clearance and the measured collider centre/extents for the planar prediction. Include every selected car and report dropped records.

The preregistered first one-second calibration targets are p95 position error ≤0.10 m, maximum ≤0.25 m, p95 speed error ≤0.25 m/s, p95 heading error ≤1 degree, and maximum underestimated body offset ≤0.10 m, over at least 100 eligible windows. These test initial model usefulness; they do not grant a 0.25 m road allowance. Future path acceptance needs an uncertainty margin supported by held-out model errors and must still keep the entire body within the existing road boundary.

Prototype 15 initially compared centred footprint proxies rather than actual colliders. Preserve its original report as physical-state calibration only; it cannot satisfy the actual-body geometry requirement. Prototype 16 corrects that comparison and adds the missing body-tilt domain condition. The driver remains unchanged in both. Instrumented batch runs are not real-time performance evidence.

Initial calibration is now retained: corrected field 16 passes across 22,147 eligible one-second windows, with 0.005685 m position p95, 0.009504 m maximum and 0.010308 m maximum underestimated body offset. Stock 17 passes across 935 windows, with 0.006382/0.006853 m position p95/maximum and 0.007666 m body-offset underestimate. Stock 16's original population-metadata failure remains recorded; 17 fixes `all` to select the actual field size without altering the model. Field inputs/events/results match the failed 14 race; its road departure and contacts remain unrepaired. These instrumented runs do not establish runtime performance.

### Adaptive corner bounds, prototype 21

Optional adaptive mode bounds all four collider-centre-aware corners throughout each piecewise rigid interpolation interval. Euclidean distance to the complete flat centreline bounds signed lateral offset. Since distance to a fixed set is 1-Lipschitz, endpoint maximum distances A/B and a maximum corner travel length L give the interval bound `(A+B+L)/2`. L includes translation and rotation about the actual body origin; the implementation adds 2 mm for floating-point rounding. Recursively subdivide any interval whose bound exceeds the unchanged 8 m boundary. At native-step resolution, an excess remains **uncertain**, not certified safe. Strictly constant route height is required, including rejection of tiny slopes beneath the ordinary elevation threshold.

Seven build checks include an intermediate rotation that crosses the road edge although both endpoints are clear, exhaustive flat-distance comparisons and curved/off-road sweeps on Cedar and Bayview. Six physical probes pass **72/72**, against **60/60** in dense mode. Nine dense queries per interval audit **12,960 positions** across the six probes, with no observed bound underestimate.
