## 1. The result we are building

Cars should try to complete the race quickly, exploit usable performance, take worthwhile passing opportunities and leave each other achievable room. They should carry speed through a corner when possible, lose time when blocked, and regain pace when space opens. Different positions should produce different sensible approaches to the same corner. Racing should remain understandable when watched from one car, not just look busy from above.

The player supplies observations and judges the experience. Astra owns implementation choices, diagnosis and thresholds. Player suggestions are evidence about the desired experience, not algorithms we must translate literally. A passing test suite cannot overrule visibly poor racing; a lively clip cannot excuse hidden physical advantages or repeated failures.

**Immediate deliverable:** a separate, competent, deterministic F1-inspired driver, compared with the existing driver in the same physical cars. Prove solo control, passing and a 24-car race before introducing personalities. Preserve the playable adventure throughout. No purchase or additional software is required for this experiment.

## 2. What counts as truth

There is no externally supplied perfect lap for Little Road's custom handling. We must distinguish facts, measured references and design targets.

| Authority | Use | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual production `RoadCar` motion under ordinary inputs | Acceleration, braking, steering response, achievable cornering and collision consequences | The simplified handling model is our game, not a real-car simulator. A formula alone does not capture its yaw servo and lateral correction forces |
| Surveyed track, body envelopes and ordered checkpoint events | Legal road space, progress, finish and no-cut checks | A legal checkpoint sequence alone does not prove that the whole body stayed on the road |
| Controlled physical experiments | Isolate equipment and control capability; verify whether a proposed manoeuvre is achievable | Valid only for recorded loadout, surface, geometry, start state and build |
| Fast, valid, matched reference laps | A demonstrated pace to approach or beat | Neither the current reference line nor the fastest known lap is a proven optimum |
| Human laps and moving review | Expose weak corner choices, unnecessary hesitation and unconvincing behaviour | Human input can include cuts and mistakes; footage alone cannot establish collision or timing accuracy |
| Numeric acceptance thresholds below | Make an engineering decision before tuning | These are project targets, not research findings or physical laws |

Use the shared physics for every driver. No direct position/velocity assignment, AI grip or power bonuses, future opponent inputs, result scripting or invisible resets. Recovery and pit operation remain explicit, physically consequential systems. Reference knowledge of the track is allowed; opponent intention is inferred from observed motion.

The selected laps contain 45, 35 and 59 whole-body-outside samples respectively; the session includes one recovery and one missed gate. ## 4. Chosen architecture

The published F1 2011 account separates ordinary driving from tactical track position. It describes track-width interest/danger maps, reachable-space selection and interpolation between samples; line preference need not dominate other opportunities. This is the design we will adapt, rather than importable F1 code or a proven solution for our physics. [Andrew Fray, Game AI Pro 2, chapter 18](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter18_Context_Steering_Behavior-Driven_Steering_at_the_Macro_Scale.pdf).

Our implementation below is a Little Road design proposal. The central contract is that **every tactical target must be drivable from the car's actual state**, and every speed reduction must retain its reason and limiting evidence.

| Component | Input → output | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing world snapshot | Physical car states → one consistent observed traffic view | No car receives another driver's future commands |
| Capability model | Catalogue, body, current state and measured calibration → acceleration/braking/turning envelope | Shared equipment interpretation; versioned, cached and conservative where uncertainty matters |
| Track guidance | Current route location and capability → upcoming geometry, legal width, reference speed guidance | Preview at least the measured stopping distance plus reaction/update travel; distinguish route coordinates from world-space collision checks |
| Context policy | Guidance, observed traffic and prior intent → continuous lateral target, manoeuvre intent and moving-traffic speed constraint | Find useful reachable space and make progress; no fixed left/middle/right command destinations |
| Path follower | Actual pose/velocity, continuous target and capability → steering and requested pedal | Construct a short smooth approach, account for its curvature and exit, then control through normal inputs |
| Immediate guard | Actual near-term motion and requested controls → minimum required braking override plus reason | React to credible imminent conflict; no tactical steering or routine queue management |
| Recovery / later pit strategy | Physical obstruction or service state → explicit mode and route request | Override only for a declared reason; no ordinary following classified as being stuck |

`ContextDriverPolicy` will implement the existing `IDriverPolicy.Decide` seam. `RoadCar` remains the only physical actuator. The old `TrafficDriverPolicy` remains selectable for comparison but will not sit underneath the new policy as a second tactical decision-maker. Existing recovery and immediate-safety code can be reused only where its measured behaviour meets this contract; the older candidate-29 street braking-guard fixture had a pace regression. The context-04 ordinary response now matches its safe guard-disabled follower time in both street and club cases, and context-05 exercises the guard through a separately declared emergency probe. This does not establish all safety or constraint-release gates.

### Concrete implementation rules

- First establish acceleration versus speed, matched-speed stopping distance and achievable transitions using the existing straight, U-turn and combined fixtures. Reuse the hill evidence and rerun it when shared forces change. Check stock, street and club, plus one held-out mixed loadout. Predictions must cover the actual control response, not only `DriveModel.Forces`.
- Track data provides legal body-centre intervals after subtracting body width and the measured control margin. Use the body shape/envelope in collision checks. Longitudinal route order helps find neighbours; world-space envelopes handle nearby but differently oriented road segments.
- Tactical value is expected useful route progress and corner-exit speed, with an explicit cost for disruptive changes. The guide line breaks near-ties. Start with one declared lookahead and a small fixed map, then vary resolution as a diagnostic: doubled resolution must not substantially change which opportunity is chosen. Resolution samples must not become prescribed driving lanes.
- Determine whether an offset can be reached from current heading, lateral motion, steering response and available distance. Validate the interpolated target and its swept approach, not merely the sampled endpoint. An empty gap on the other side of an occupied car is not immediately reachable.
- A moving leader imposes a moving speed/gap constraint when necessary. Its presence alone does not imply a stop. A closing encounter, adjacent overlap and a stationary obstacle have different consequences. Longer-horizon uncertainty informs opportunity choice; uncertainty alone must not force immediate maximum braking.
- Build a speed envelope for the selected approach and the next corner exit. An inside line can require lower entry speed; an outside approach can trade distance for exit speed. If a pass ceases to fit, yield or fall in behind without steering through the other body. Do not suppress normal corner braking to reduce a headline brake percentage.
- Hold a useful manoeuvre while its benefit and clearance remain. Record an explicit abort or completion when changing it. Apply bounded smoothing to preference, but refresh physical danger immediately; smoothing must not delay emergency response.
- When no pass fits, follow at an attainable speed and keep searching. When genuinely boxed in, waiting is valid. Report deadlock when measurable feasible forward space exists and the driver fails to use it; do not force movement through an obstacle merely to satisfy a timer.
- Initial tactical updates: staggered 10 Hz, preserving the existing 120 Hz physics/control path. Reuse buffers and a shared neighbour search. No per-frame scene-wide allocation or unbounded all-path search. These rates are starting engineering choices and must satisfy the performance gate.

The low-level path/speed representation is independently testable. Racing-track representations and controller responsibilities are also covered in [Game AI Pro, chapter 39](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter39_Representing_and_Driving_a_Race_Track_for_AI_Controlled_Vehicles.pdf); our actual measured response decides whether the chosen follower works here.

## 5. Acceptance measures and exact interpretation

**Hard invariants:** finite bounded controls, unchanged shared forces, valid ordered gates, no hidden resets, complete result accounting, no collision-query saturation, and no measured body penetration above the existing 0.02 m tolerance. Sampled clearance does not prove mathematically continuous non-clipping; use physics-step overlap checks and incident sweeps together. Controlled no-error fixtures require zero contacts, departures and recovery. A crash in a full field remains visible even if it has a reasonable explanation.

| Measure | Initial gate fixed before implementation | Meaning / safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeatability | Three identical runs: completion-time range ≤0.5% of median, same completion/contact verdict | If not, diagnose nondeterminism or fixture instability before interpreting small policy differences; not a cross-machine determinism promise |
| Capability prediction | Held-out speed/entry cases: acceleration time and stopping distance within 10% of measurement; a braking envelope must not underestimate required distance by more than 5% | Conservative overprediction still has to pass the pace tests; inspect errors by speed band rather than averaging them away |
| Clear-road competence | Three valid laps per stock/street/club setup; median flying pace no more than 3% slower than the fastest verified clean matched reference available | Starting lap scored separately. A faster reference found later raises the evidence standard; refresh only with matched provenance |
| Irrelevant traffic | Receding car, distant parallel car and safely trailing car: ≤0.5 s added completion time and ≤0.5 percentage points additional braking against matched solo | Three repeats per case. Controls need not be identical; sustained evasive movement needs an identified conflict |
| Ordinary following | No collision or unforced stop below 1 m/s while leader remains above 5 m/s and there is forward room; no greater than 0.5 s guard-induced delay over matched guard-disabled safe control | Retain the existing street/club braking comparison; disabling the guard is a diagnostic, not the shipping fix |
| Available pass | Faster follower completes a sustained pass in each deliberately feasible controlled case, within 1.25 × the physical witness manoeuvre time + 1 s | Establish witness and cutoff before candidate tuning; no arbitrary deadline shorter than acceleration and closing distance permit |
| Side-by-side corner | Both cars finish the scored section, with no contact/cut/reset; preserve existing Cedar corner deadlines of 16 s street / 14 s club in those existing fixtures | A clean yield can pass a contested-corner test; an overtaking fixture separately requires a completed pass |
| Emergency brake | All avoidable hard-brake/stationary probes avoid contact; additional speed-limiting request releases within 0.25 s after its constraint demonstrably clears | Corner constraints may remain. Also test a physically unavoidable start condition: grade mitigation and honest attribution, not impossible avoidance |
| Unproductive brake cycling | At least 25% fewer audited unexplained brake cycles per car-km than matched candidate 29, with no slower median lap or worse incident distribution | If baseline count is zero, require zero. Freeze baseline event count before tuning; fewer cycles cannot compensate for a safety failure |
| Tactical indecision | Zero unexplained left-right-left or right-left-right intent reversals within 2 s in controlled stable encounters | Count manoeuvre intent relative to road, not wheel steering corrections or an S-bend; log a changed hazard/benefit for legitimate reversals |

A **brake episode** has requested pedal below −0.05 for at least 0.2 s. An accelerate episode exceeds +0.2 for at least 0.2 s. A brake cycle is brake–accelerate–brake with the second braking episode starting within 2 s of the first ending. Report both total cycles and the audited unexplained subset. Valid reasons include measured corner-speed limits, a closing body, insufficient stopping distance and recovery. Merely labelling a command “traffic” does not explain it: retain the limiting object, distance, speed bound and predicted conflict. Use interval-weighted durations; disclose trace gaps.

Line adherence is time within ±1 m of the reference, reported separately for clear running, following, side-by-side, passing and recovery. Higher adherence is useful in a matched clear-track comparison; it is **not a full-field optimisation target**. Record brake duty by the same contexts, actual/target speed, sector time and lost time versus matched solo. Longitudinal progress and completion matter more than matching a painted line.

## 6. Test ladder and independent checks

Each rung has a falsifiable expected outcome. Do not skip from a tidy solo lap to a production replacement.

| Rung | Cases | Independent check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: capability and clear road | Existing acceleration/braking/U-turn/combined courses; Cedar stock/street/club; mixed loadout held out | Scripted physical inputs isolate car capability. Clean existing follower supplies a competing pace reference; measure actual Rigidbody motion |
| B: harmless nearby traffic | No car; behind/receding; parallel outside swept path; leader too distant to affect the section | Paired solo difference. Identical surroundings except for the observed car; judge whether any braking/movement has physical justification |
| C: necessary response | Constant slower leader, braking leader, stationary obstacle, narrowing corridor, boxed-in start | Analytic closing-distance estimate plus measured stopping envelope. Include both avoidable and explicitly impossible encounters |
| D: usable passing | Faster follower/slow leader; reversed-speed three-car queue; mirrored left/right opportunities; gap closes mid-attempt | First prove a legal witness manoeuvre through ordinary controls. Swap car identities/grid sides; verify pass persists ≥2 s with the full body clear |
| E: corner racecraft | Inside/outside two-wide, three cars entering a corner, late approach, blocked inside, linked turns | Score entry/apex/exit separately. Demonstrate room and braking feasibility before requiring an overtake; no reward for a collision-assisted pass |
| F: recovery | Sideways/off-road/wrong-way car, wall, missed gate, approaching traffic during rejoin, queue lasting >6 s | Gate integrity; physically clear rejoin; no reset hidden as successful driving; normal queue must not trigger reverse recovery |
| G: field | Fixed 24-car Cedar benchmark, then genuine grid/loadout variations and Bayview | All-car checkpoint/finish accounting, incidents, sustained passes, pace distribution and moving review |

The witness for D is a measured clean manoeuvre using ordinary car controls, such as a scripted smooth lateral approach, not an analytically prescribed position trajectory. For cases where no witness or measured safe opportunity exists, do not require a pass simply because a car has a higher performance rating. Keep these as follow/yield cases until feasibility is established.

Regression comparisons use the same car identities, loadouts and initial states. Hold-out cases alter encounter distance/speed, side, body envelope and mixed equipment—not just an unused random seed. Mirroring is useful but does not substitute for new corner geometry. Reserve a Bayview run and a modified encounter set until the initial prototype decision; failures there become reported generalisation failures, not silently absorbed training cases.

First screen with three repeats per controlled case and one full field. Before promotion, use 20 paired field scenarios with genuinely varied grids/equipment and multiple laps, distributed across Cedar and Bayview. Set the scenario manifest before tuning and retain every failure. This is an initial coverage budget, not proof of a rare-crash rate. Report per-race results and incidents per car-hour, including severity and uncertainty; never average unfinished cars out of pace statistics.

## 7. Full-field contract

A missing finisher makes the full total incomplete; never omit a slow/DNF car or substitute zero. Corner fixtures use the scored exit and disclose 10 Hz timing precision; standing races use official finish times after green. Keep collision/departure verdicts beside the time comparison so an unsafe shortcut cannot earn acceptance.
