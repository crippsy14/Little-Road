# 86. Vinelands, Zone 1: the Largo and Vila Velha (brief, 21 October 2026)

This is the first zone of the rebuild, from the town plan (`review/street-review/vinelands-town-plan.md`, sections 1–4
and 7) and docs/85's zone order. Frame: `a` metres east, `n` north of the Largo's middle, world (3400, −3280), at about
150 m. It's built behind `--vinelands`.

## What this place is, and why it's here
The oldest part of town. A chapel stood where a cattle route crossed the ridge track, and the houses crowded round it
between the 12th and 15th centuries. They sat on the granite crest, where the ground is dry and defensible, next to the
spring on the west side. They faced east, sheltered from the Atlantic wind and catching the morning sun.

It's the town's front room: the church, the town hall, the cafés, the post office, the bank. And it's its densest,
oldest housing. About 750 people live here, and many houses are shut up (owned by emigrants). It must read as **old,
tight, granite and whitewash, stepping down the slope**, the opposite of the new villas on the Mirante or the 1980s
flats at the Portela.

## Shape
The Largo is 70 × 45 m, long axis north–south, centred at (−5, 0). Vila Velha fans out east and south-east of it, down
the top of the amphitheatre. Its outline (from the plan):
(−75, −100) (−75, 100) (30, 140) (180, 110) (240, 10) (220, −110) (100, −130) (−10, −120). About 7 ha, a lopsided
fan: wide at the Largo, pinched at the east tip where the slope steepens, cut on the north-east by the convent garden
wall.

## Architecture
- **Houses:** narrow row houses with party walls, on 5–7 m frontages and 12–20 m deep plots.
  - 2–3 storeys; the ground floor is often a store or workshop, homes above.
  - They meet the street directly, with no front gardens.
  - Each house steps down with the slope on its own floor level, so the eaves and sills stagger along a lane.
- **Materials:**
  - Exposed granite at corners, door and window frames, and the ground floor of older houses; whitewash above.
  - Some fronts tiled in azulejo (blue and white, green, or ochre patterns); the odd ochre or rose wash.
  - Roman tile roofs, mostly two-pitch with the ridge along the street; a few hipped corner houses.
  - Chimneys with little tile hoods.
- **Details that make it read as lived in:**
  - wrought-iron balconies with pots;
  - wooden varandas (galleries) on the downhill side;
  - wall-mounted lanterns, not lamp posts, in the lanes;
  - laundry lines across the narrow lanes, and shutters half open;
  - an oratório (a little street shrine) in a wall;
  - doors in bottle green, ruby, tile blue and chestnut.
- **On the Largo:**
  - the church (Matriz) on the east side, its façade to the square: granite pilasters and a single bell tower;
  - the town hall (Paços do Concelho) on the north side: three storeys, a granite arcade at street level, a clock and a
    flag;
  - the post office and the bank on the west side, with shopfronts;
  - the Café Central at the south-west corner, with a terrace of tables and parasols;
  - the pastry shop and the pharmacy (green cross sign) on the south side.

## Streets
- **The Largo:** black-and-white cobbles (calçada portuguesa) in a wave pattern, the fountain (chafariz) at (−5, −15),
  plane trees on the long sides, and benches under them.
- **Drivable lanes** (3–4.5 m, one-way feel, kerbless cobbles): Rua da Igreja (30,45) (100,70) (170,80) (215,40); Rua
  do Forno (−10,−35) (50,−75) (120,−100) (200,−100); Rua do Poço (40,−20) (90,−60) (110,−110).
- **Pedestrian only:**
  - Travessa dos Tanoeiros (60,45) (80,95) (60,125), ending in the Pátio dos Tanoeiros (15 × 15 m, at (60,125));
  - Beco do Cego (120,−100) (150,−60) (165,−30), 2 m wide;
  - the Pátio do Forno (18 × 14 m, at (115,−105)): the communal bread oven and a water tank.
- **Stepped lanes (escadinhas):** do Mirante (60,10) (110,−20) (150,−70) (190,−120); da Cerca (110,70) (160,40) (210,5)
  (260,−25); da Fonte (−60,−40) (−110,−30) (−150,−10).
- **The top of the Calçada Velha** (the old mule steps to the river) leaves from (60, −20).
- **Where the four roads enter the Largo:**
  - Rua Direita from the north;
  - the Calçada (Estrada do Cais) from the south-east corner;
  - the Mirante road from the south-west;
  - the Caminho Velho do Vale from the west.

## Features (the things you remember)
1. The Largo with its fountain, plane trees and wave cobbles: the town's living room.
2. The church front and its tower over the square. Behind it, the churchyard terrace (adro) looks east over the whole
   amphitheatre to the river: the town's best balcony.
3. The town hall clock facing the church across the square.
4. The Pátio do Forno: bread smoke at dawn, the oven's arch, a bench, a cat-sized window.
5. Laundry over the Rua do Poço.
6. The escadinhas: stone steps with a handrail, pots on every landing, houses stepping down beside them.
7. The oratório with a candle and fresh flowers, at the corner of the Rua do Forno and the Beco do Cego.
8. The Largo da Fonte at the zone's west edge (−150, −10): the spring, a granite trough and the washing tanks
   (lavadouro) under a tile roof, and a lime tree.

## Life (cars only: drivers are implied)
- **Morning:** a delivery van at the north side of the Largo before 9:30; the bread van leaving the Pátio do Forno; old
  regulars' cars parked outside the Café Central.
- **Midday:** the bells; quiet; a car or two crawling the Rua da Igreja.
- **Evening:** cars parked round the Largo's edge; café terraces busy (lit); a slow loop of cruising cars.
- **Tuesday and Thursday:** the fish van on the Largo.
- **Named residents:** the café owner, the priest (at the church), the baker (at the Pátio do Forno), the town hall's
  clerk. All cars, parked where they live and work.

## Edges
- **North:** Rua Direita's façades continue the townscape (Zone 2).
- **North-east:** the Jardim da Cerca's wall (Zone 2).
- **East and south-east:** the houses thin into the Courelas de Cima smallholdings, with garden walls, sheds and figs
  (Zone 4).
- **South-west:** the Mirante road, where the houses loosen into villas (Zone 5).
- **West:** the Largo da Fonte and the lip down to the Eiras (Zone 7).

   - a shop or workshop below;
   - a downhill house with a wooden varanda;
   - a tall three-storey house;
   - a shut-up house (closed shutters, a faded wash).

3. **The town hall:** a ground-floor arcade, a clock and a flag.

## Build rounds (21 October)
Each round: build, audit, photograph the zone's nine views, fix, repeat. Photos are deleted once checked.
1. **Rows and the Largo.** The `vila` kit v1 (car-forge): six row-house families, the town hall, the fountain, the
   washing tanks, the oven. Rows on party walls along the lanes, each house on its own floor with a granite plinth.
   The Largo reads as a square but was patchy, with gravel strips across it.
2. **Paving and lanes.** One drivable calçada surface across the whole Largo (a generated wave texture), granite setts
   on the lanes, the escadinhas with steps, wall lanterns, laundry, the shrine.
3. **Gaps and gables (kit v3).** Side windows on every gable. A refused house leaves a granite garden wall along the
   street, not open grass.
4. **The back yards and life (kit v4).** Behind each house, its own walled quintal as deep as the block allows: vine
   pergola, kitchen plot of couves, fig or orange tree, shed, hens, tank; a shut-up house's yard has gone to a fig.
   The churchyard's cross and cypresses. Cars parked round the Largo's edge; five named residents (Padre Américo,
   Dona Fátima at the town hall, Sr. Joaquim at the Café Central, Dona Rosa at the oven, Tia Lurdes at the washing
   tanks); five cruising cars on Rua Direita, the Calçada, the Mirante road and the river road.
5. **Gardens in the gaps.** A gap in a row is a garden plot behind its street wall (plots, an orchard of figs and
   oranges, a shed). Yard walls reach the ground on slopes. The plaza's height grid takes a 3 × 3 median (a ray caught
   a thin edge and folded the paving into teeth).
6. **Walls never close a way.** A garden wall breaks where it meets a lane, a stair, a square or a road verge. Trees
   stand on sloping yards.

7–11. **The Largo's paving, made clean.** Teeth and a strip across the square, found layer by layer: the road shoulders'
   gravel (now stopped inside the paved square, `VinelandsVila.Paved`, and no kerb or mouth shoulders at its junction),
   then the junction's paint (5 cm proud of its asphalt; found with the new `--list-renderers-near x,z,r` probe). The
   paving now stands 15 cm over everything under it, ramped at 12 %, easing to 3 cm over its last 4 m.
12. **The saddle junction.** Rua Direita left the saddle 33° off the entry road's arm, and the junction overlapped itself
   (steps of 1.5 m). It now leaves heading south, square to it; the country's seams fell from 20 to 1.
13. **Flush edges.** The paving meets each road leaving the square flush (it had carried its raised middle to the edge).

Audit after round 13 (the town region, 460 m round the Largo, still holds unbuilt zones 2–5): residents 4, parked 15,
