# Car Forge r17 review (banger, hatch, wedge)

Reviewed all 20 images in review/forge/r17/ (3 cars x 6 views, plus lineup-34front and lineup-side). Round 1 is review/forge/review-r12.md. The nose, flank and top cameras are framed for the wedge, so the banger and hatch are cropped or off-centre in those views (the banger and hatch top views cut off the roof or tail).

Scores against the target (premium die-cast toy, chunky, clean, varied, tiers visible):

| Car | r12 | r17 |
|---|---|---|
| test_banger | 4.5 | **5.5** |
| test_hatch | 4 | **5.5** |
| test_wedge | 5 | **6** |

The round's work gave the biggest gains in glass, arches and silhouette (hatch). The surfacing is still mostly hairline decals on smooth blobs. Colour is still pastel, and the chrome and wheel materials still read as flat grey.

---

## 1. test_banger (class C, Eastern Bloc saloon) — 5.5/10

### Round-1 issues now FIXED
- **White crescent over the wheels:** gone. The arches are dark and closed, with a curled lip. In the flank view the arch reads as a clean rounded dark rim.
- **Glass:** much better. It is lighter and the interior reads (dash, two seat backs, steering wheel, wipers) in the 34front, nose and flank views. The window surrounds have a chrome rim with thickness.
- **Stance:** lower and humbler. The tyres no longer leave a big gap under the body. The steel wheels have a dish and a chrome ring, and they read as period.
- **Lamps:** the indicators are bigger (orange blocks below the headlamps). The round headlamps with chrome rims and the slatted chrome oval grille hold up.
- **Bumper:** thicker, and it wraps round at the corners. The 34front view shows the profile.
- **Exhaust:** now visible at the rear (a small pipe below the bumper, left of centre).
- **Mirrors:** bigger, with a visible stalk and head.
- **Door handle:** now a real pull shape. Side trim runs the full length.
- **Cabin blend:** better. The tumblehome and a soft roof edge make the cabin look less bolted on. In the 34rear view the cabin is still a narrower box sitting on a deep, flat rear deck, but it now reads as a tumblehome saloon, not a lid on a shelf.

### Round-1 issues that REMAIN
- **Paint:** pale teal, near-matte, and it still sits close in value to the grey studio. In lineup-34front it is the weakest silhouette against the background. It has a faint clearcoat sheen on the roof and bonnet, but no depth, no two-tone and no wear. It reads as a clean pastel toy, not a cheap old car.
- **Bonnet:** the relief ridges are not visible in any view. The bonnet is still one soft slab with a bulge at the front edge (a small lump above the grille in the nose view). There is no shut line, no centre crease and no grille surround.
- **Door shut lines:** still black hairlines. The panel is not raised or inset.
- **Rear plate:** now a row of four small white boxes (34rear). It reads as a texture glitch, not a plate. The front plate is a white sliver under the bumper.
- **Rear deck:** still a big flat slab between the rear glass and the lamps.
- **Bumper:** a flat mid-grey, not a chrome-bright metal. It does not reflect the studio, so it reads as grey plastic.

### New faults from this round
- **Arch feet:** in the side view the dark arch liner ends in square black feet that poke below the sill line at all four corners. They look like mudflaps and break the clean lower edge.
- **Grille speckle:** the grille slats have white noise and sparkle along the top edge (nose view). It looks like z-fighting between the grille insert and its surround.
- **Wheels:** the hubcap is a flat dark disc with a chrome centre. From the side the dark wheel and black tyre merge, so the rim only reads through the thin chrome ring.

### Next improvements (most impactful first)
1. **Paint.** Push it off the studio grey. Try a deeper period colour (faded mustard, brick, a darker teal). Add a visible two-tone (roof or lower sill in cream or dark) and a slightly darker lower body. Keep the clearcoat sheen on the bonnet and roof only.
2. **Bonnet.** Add two raised edge creases or a centre ridge, a real shut line with a dark inset, and a body-coloured grille surround with a lip. Fix the lump above the grille by making the front edge a single smooth curve.
3. **Panels with depth.** Make the door lines a 2 mm dark groove with the door panel slightly raised, and add a hinge line at the A-pillar. Add end caps to the side trim.
4. **Plates.** Replace the four-box rear plate with one rounded rectangle in a shallow recess. Do the same for the front plate and give it a bracket above the bumper.
5. **Chrome.** Give the bumpers, grille rim and mirror heads a true metallic material (high metallic, low roughness) so they pick up the studio. Add over-riders with a tip.
6. **Arch feet and wheels.** Trim the arch liners flush to the sill. Put a bright face or lip on the hubcap so the wheel separates from the tyre.
7. **Rear deck.** Add a boot-lid shut line, a small lip spoiler or a chrome boot handle, and a badge pad. This breaks up the flat shelf.
8. **Grille.** Fix the z-fighting by insetting the slats 1 mm behind the surround.

## 2. test_hatch (class C family hatch, Golf Mk1 / Renault 5 spirit) — 5.5/10

### Round-1 issues now FIXED
- **Silhouette:** this is the biggest improvement. In the side view the roof now slopes into a raked tailgate, the roof is lower, and the car reads as a hatchback, not a van. The 34rear view shows a large raked rear window with a wiper.
- **Wheels:** the flat four-spoke cross is replaced with a five-spoke alloy with a rim lip and a hub, and the sidewalls are thinner. They read much better than r12.
- **White crescent, black fin and dark vanes:** the crescent and the black fin in front of each tyre are gone. The arches are closed and dark.
- **Rear wiper:** now present.
- **Glass:** lighter, with a warm interior that reads through (seat backs, wheel, roof lining). The black surrounds give a clear shape.
- **Mirrors:** bigger, body-coloured, with a visible stalk.
- **Colour:** salmon-orange is distinct from the banger and the wedge. This is a real gain for variety.
- **Door handle and rubbing strip:** both are present and read well.

### Round-1 issues that REMAIN
- **Bonnet relief:** the bonnet has a hairline shut line (a rectangle plus the swoop) but no ridges. The surface is a smooth slab with a sheen.
- **Front face:** the lamp housings are still large, and the grille is a black slab with a thin body-coloured rim. The plate sits on the grille edge as a white slab. The bumper is a plain black bar.
- **Rear:** the tail lamps are rounded rectangles with a black edge, and the bumper is a blocky shelf. The tailgate has no shut line, no handle and no badge.
- **Body side:** still one rubbing strip and door hairlines. There is no shoulder crease, no sill shape and no C-pillar identity (the rear quarter is a plain thick pillar).
- **Mirrors and tyre shoulder:** the tyres have no chamfer. The sidewalls are still slightly tall for a hot-hatch feel.

### New faults from this round
- **Black tag:** in the 34front view a dark wedge sticks out below the front arch, forward of the wheel (the front end of the rubbing strip or an arch end). It reads as a mudflap or a fin. This is a form of the round-1 black fin.
- **Arch feet:** same square black feet below the sill as the banger (side view, both wheels).
- **Grille rim:** the thin rim around the grille shows speckle noise in the nose view.
- **Rear plate:** the same four-box plate as the banger.
- **Hub:** the five-spoke alloy shows a square key at the hub, and the spokes are flat grey with no bevel.
- **Proportion read:** in the lineup the hatch looks longer and heavier than the banger. Class C should read small and light. A kerb-weight cue is missing.

### Next improvements (most impactful first)
1. **Tailgate.** Add a shut line around the glass and a handle or badge pad. Add a short roof spoiler lip above the glass. Shape the bumper with end returns so it stops reading as a shelf.
2. **Front face.** Shrink the lamp housings about 10%, enlarge the grille and give it a body-coloured surround. Put the plate in a recess below the grille or on the bumper, with a clean edge.
3. **Bonnet.** Add two raised side creases running from the A-pillars to the lamps and one centre crease. The shut line should become a real inset.
4. **Body side.** Add a beltline crease (a soft ridge one third of the way up the door), a shaped sill, and a small flare over each arch. Give the C-pillar a black panel or a visible quarter window so it has identity.
5. **Remove the black tag.** End the rubbing strip inside the door line with a rounded end cap, and trim the arch feet flush to the sill.
6. **Wheels.** Bevel the spokes and add a lip chamfer. Make the face a brighter metal than the grey. Put a shoulder chamfer on the tyre.
7. **Tail lamps.** Add a split lens (red plus amber), an inset reverse lamp, and a recess.
8. **Size.** Shorten the car about 5% or lower the roof 3% so it reads smaller than the banger or equal to it.

## 3. test_wedge (class A 1970s wedge supercar) — 6/10

### Round-1 issues now FIXED
- **Nose length and height:** in the side view the nose is now a low, long straight taper that ends in a point, much lower than r12. The flat line from the arch to the nose tip reads as a wedge.
- **Pop-up lamp lids:** the black vanes and fins are gone. They are now flush outlined quads with thin shut lines. They read as closed lamps (see the 34front and top views).
- **Number plate on the nose:** removed. The blurry blob is gone.
- **Indicator:** now a small flat orange pill, not a long one.
- **Splitter:** now a thin black slit with a chrome edge, much less chewed. (It reads as a small smiling mouth, but it is clean.)
- **Wing:** lower and smaller, and the endplates are body-coloured. In the 34rear view it sits on the tail with two thin black posts.
- **Rear tyres:** wider than the front (see the 34rear and top views). The stagger is visible.
- **Engine louvres:** present on the rear deck (the top view shows the black ribbed cover at the tail). The rear deck is no longer empty.
- **Rear face:** the tail grille, two long red lamps with white reversing lamps beside them, and two exhaust pipes read as an A-class tail. The pipes now stick out below the bumper line as chrome tips, which reads better.
- **White crescent:** gone. The arches are dark and closed.
- **Glass:** the best of the three. The interior has seats, wheel and an orange-brown dash lining. The side window has a clean quad shape. The screen frame is thinner than r12, though still heavy at the base.
- **Side scoop:** a good detail, now an oval with black slats and a raised lip.

### Round-1 issues that REMAIN
- **Bonnet saddle:** from the front, the bonnet is a fender hump, then a dip, then the cowl (see the 34front and nose views and the flank view). The front arches rise as lumps on either side of a hollow, and the lamp quads sit on the humps. The side view shows this as a concave dip between the nose and the screen. The brief was a straight rise to the screen, and this is not yet a straight line. It reads as a sag and a pinched waist, not as a wedge.
- **Plan view:** the nose is a wide blunt spade (34front and nose views) even though the side profile is pointed. A wedge also needs a plan taper toward the nose.
- **Front flares:** the flare edge round the front arch is still ragged (small notches on the rear side of the flare in 34front and the nose view), and it sits on the body without a smooth blend. The rear flares are smoother.
- **Wing endplates:** in the side view they are a flat orange block with a black post and a gap. The wing is smaller but still reads as a box stuck on.
- **Paint:** mustard-orange. It is better lit, but still dull for an A-class car. The shoulder highlight is soft.
- **Windscreen base:** the thick black cowl bar still reads as a heavy shelf.
- **Mirrors:** tiny black stubs.
- **Black parts:** sills, flares, posts and wheels are all the same matte black, with no accent or chrome.

### New faults from this round
- **Arch feet:** square black feet below the sill at all four arches (side view, both wheels at both ends).
- **Heavy arch flange:** in the side view the dark flange around each arch is thick and uniform, and it reads as a black crescent bolted on. It is the loudest feature in the side view.
- **Wheel contrast:** the wheels are dark grey spokes on a black tyre with a thin chrome lip. Against the studio, the wheels read as black discs with a bright ring, and the spokes are lost.
- **Lamp quads:** the flush lamp lids are outlined squares only. They look engraved or like decals. The left one is skewed.
- **Side scoop slats:** the slats are black bars with a cross at the front (the "+" and "III" in the oval). It reads as text ("HII") from the 34front view, not as a vent.
- **Rear tyre height:** the rear tyre top sits above the rear deck line in the side view, so the tail looks buried in the wheel.

### Next improvements (most impactful first)
1. **Bonnet line.** Remove the saddle. Make the bonnet one continuous surface from the nose tip to the windscreen base, with the fender crests as soft ridges only 2 or 3% above it. The side view should show a single straight rise.
2. **Plan taper.** Narrow the nose to about 70% of the cabin width at the tip, with a visible leading-edge crease that carries back along the fenders.
3. **Pop-up lamps.** Raise the lids slightly (2 to 3 mm) with a visible hinge line and a dark gap, or show one lamp up. They should read as hardware, not drawn lines.
4. **Front flare.** Rebuild as an even-lip extrusion that blends into the fender with no notches, and make the arch top lower than the fender crest.
5. **Wheels.** Brighten the spoke face (satin silver or gold) so the five-spoke design reads against the tyre. Add a polished lip and a tyre shoulder chamfer. Lower the rear tyre height a touch or raise the rear deck so the tyre top sits below the deck line.
6. **Paint.** Go to a saturated orange or yellow with a metallic flake and a strong shoulder highlight. Add a racing stripe or a roof panel in a second colour.
7. **Wing.** Make the endplates rounded with a foot that sits on the tail, use thicker posts or a single centre pylon, and give the blade an airfoil edge.
8. **Side scoop and cowl.** Make the scoop a recessed duct with darker depth and fewer, longer slats (no "+"). Thin the windscreen cowl bar and add a body-coloured scuttle. Enlarge the mirrors.

## Tier-difference verdict

The wedge now reads as a different class from the two C cars within one second in both lineup views: low nose, wide rear stance, wing, louvres, five-spoke dark wheels, and a tinted-interior cabin. The step C to A is clear.

The banger vs hatch step is also readable, for the first time: the hatch has an alloy five-spoke, a black plastic bumper, a raked tailgate and a modern face, while the banger has steel wheels, a chrome bumper and a three-box shape. That is a believable "old to newer" step inside class C, and the colours (teal, salmon, mustard) separate them.

What still weakens the tiers:
- **All three share the same surface language:** smooth gloss body, hairline shut lines, a flat rubbing strip, and black arch flanges with square feet. The wedge differs by shape, not by detail count or finish.
- **Colour:** all three are pastel and low-contrast against the grey studio. A class A car should look more saturated and glossier than a class C banger. Today the value difference between the cars is small.
- **Materials:** chrome is flat grey, wheel faces are dark, and glass is the same tint level. There is nothing that says "better materials".
- **The B-class gap:** no parameter yet separates a mid-tier, such as arch flare size, tyre stagger, chrome vs black trim, or two-tone paint.

## Top 5 generator-level changes now

1. **Arch and sill clean-up pass (shared builder).** Trim the arch liner flush to the sill so the four square feet disappear. Make the flange thinner and tier-scaled (thin on the banger, medium on the hatch, a smooth continuous flare on the wedge). End the rubbing strip and sill with rounded caps that stop short of the arch, so the hatch black tag goes. Rebuild the wedge front flare as an even extrusion with no notches.
2. **Panels with real depth.** Replace hairline shut lines with an inset groove and a slightly raised panel (door, bonnet, tailgate, boot lid). Add bonnet creases or ridges as geometry, not decals (banger and hatch both still lack them), and make the wedge lamp lids and scoop slats physical recesses. This is the main reason the cars still read as smooth blobs.
3. **Paint and material tiering.** Retire the pastels (also Richard's standing preference for less pastel). Class C: deeper, slightly worn colours with an optional two-tone. Class A: saturated, metallic flake, strong clearcoat. Make chrome a true metallic material so bumpers, grille rims and mirror heads pick up the studio. Darken the studio floor or add a gradient so pale cars (the banger) separate from the background.
4. **Wheel and tyre library.** Keep the five-spoke, but add a bright face or polished lip, bevelled spokes, and a tyre shoulder chamfer. Make the face colour tier-driven (painted steel, silver alloy, satin gold or bronze, machined), so dark-spoke wheels on black tyres stop reading as black discs. Fix the rear tyre vs deck-line height on stagger cars.
5. **Face and rear kit.** Replace the four-box plate with one recessed rounded plate (front and rear). Inset the grille slats to stop the z-fighting speckle. Build tail lamps as split lenses with a recess, and give each archetype one rule that the generator enforces: the wedge keeps a single straight bonnet line with a plan taper, the hatch keeps a tailgate shut line with a roof lip, and the banger keeps a bonnet ridge with a body-coloured grille surround.
