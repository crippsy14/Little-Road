# Car Forge r12 review (banger, hatch, wedge)

Reviewed all 18 images in review/forge/r12/. Target: premium die-cast toy look (chunky, rounded, clean, readable), varied cars, clear tier steps.

Scores against that target: banger 4.5/10, hatch 4/10, wedge 5/10. The "about 20%" self-estimate is fair for finish and fidelity, but the silhouettes are further along than the surfacing, materials and details.

---

## 1. test_banger (class C, Eastern Bloc saloon) — 4.5/10

Current read: a recognisable slab-sided Trabant/Wartburg-ish three-box. Best silhouette idea of the three for its class. Weakest on colour, material and surface depth.

Improvements, most impactful first:

1. **Paint and contrast.** The pale grey-green body, grey ground and grey sky nearly merge in the 3/4 shots. The paint is one flat matte value with no depth. Use a stronger, slightly dirty period colour (mustard, brick red, faded teal) with a clear specular. A banger can still be saturated; "cheap" should come from shape and wear, not from a washed-out colour. Add a visible two-tone option (roof or lower sill in a different colour) and a little weathering (a darker lower body, a faded bonnet and roof) to make the "old" read.
2. **Greenhouse looks bolted on.** In the rear 3/4 and side views the cabin is a separate box sitting on a shelf. The base of the A-pillar, C-pillar and rear window meets the body with no blend or fillet. There is a big flat rear deck behind the glass, and the roof edge is a hard rectangle. Add a soft shoulder/beltline fillet where the cabin meets the body, a small rear-window rake, and a slight tumblehome. The top view shows the side glass as thin slits outside the roof edge, so the roof is narrower than the glazing. That reads as a box lid and not as body.
3. **Bonnet is a flat slab.** It is one tilted plane with a pinched nose and no crease. Add a shallow centre ridge or two raised edge creases running from the headlamps to the windscreen base, and a visible bonnet shut line. The front face drops straight from the lip with no grille surround or valance. Give the grille a body-coloured surround and a bonnet-lip overhang.
4. **Wheels and stance.** The tyres are tall and round, with tiny dished hubcaps, and the arches are plain cut-outs with no lip. The body sits a bit high with a big gap above the tyre, which looks cartoon-van rather than slow old saloon. Lower the body 5-8%, add a rolled arch lip (a thin raised rim in body colour), and use bigger, flatter steel-wheel dishes with a bolder chrome or painted rim. The ortho side view shows a bright white crescent above each wheel (the arch liner or lip catching light) that looks like a rendering fault. Make the liner dark.
3b. (Same area) The wheelbase and overhangs look right, but the wheels are small relative to the box, which is good for the era. Do not enlarge the tyres; widen the track a little so the body does not look so narrow over the wheels.
5. **Side detail is decal-thin.** The door shut lines are hairlines and the chrome strip is a flat line. Make shut lines a real groove with a slightly raised door panel, give the door handle a proper pull shape, and let the trim strip bulge. A rubbing strip with end caps would help.
6. **Front and rear faces.** The grille is a decent chrome slatted oval and the round headlamps with chrome rims read well. The weak points are the tiny amber lamps (too small for the face; increase 50%) and the thin chrome bumper blade that just floats at the corners with stubby over-riders. Add a proper bumper profile (thicker, with a centre dip and overriders). Rear: the round red lamps are good; the number plate with a "bumpy" top edge looks messy, so give it a clean rectangle and a recess.
7. **Glass is too opaque and dark.** The windows are a uniform dark grey slab with a barely visible interior (seats just visible as dark blobs). Lighten the glass tint and add a visible, lighter interior (dash line, two simple seat shapes, a steering wheel) so the cabin has depth. Add a thin chrome/rubber window surround that has some thickness.
8. **Small details.** Wing mirrors are tiny and look stuck on the A-pillar base; give them a bracket and a larger, rounder head. Add fuel cap, a roof drip rail, and an aerial or a single wiper pair that is more visible. Exhaust is not visible from the rear (tail pipe missing).

Class read: it reads old and slab-sided, but not clearly slow or cheap. The tall wheels, high stance and clean unmarked paint make it look more like a tidy retro toy than a wreck. Add wear and a lower, humbler stance.

## 2. test_hatch (class C family hatch, Golf Mk1 / Panda spirit) — 4/10

Current read: a boxy tall estate/van-ish car. The side profile does not read as a hatchback; it reads as a short wagon crossed with a Lada Niva. Nothing identifies it as Golf or Panda.

Improvements, most impactful first:

1. **Silhouette: it is not a hatch.** From the side the roof runs flat to a near-vertical rear with only a small rounded corner, so there is no hatch slope, and the rear glass looks like a van back. A Golf/Panda hatch needs a defined, raked tailgate (about 30-40 degrees from vertical), a lower and shorter roof relative to the body, and a visible tail "kick" or spoiler lip. The cabin is also very tall relative to the lower body (the beltline is high and the glass area is deep), which makes the car look bulky and van-like. Lower the roof about 8-10% and reduce the glasshouse depth.
2. **Wheels look cheap and flat.** The 4-spoke "cross" rim is a flat grey plus-shape with big dark gaps, with a tiny hub. It looks like a placeholder, not a premium toy. Replace it with a proper steel wheel or a rounded 5-spoke alloy with a rim lip and a visible hub, and size the rim up so the tyre sidewall is thinner. The tyre sidewalls are very tall and the tyres have no tread edge or chamfer; add a shoulder chamfer.
3. **Arch/lip rendering faults.** The ortho side view shows a white crescent outline over each wheel (the arch liner or lip rendering bright) and a black wedge fin in front of each tyre (in the 34front and nose views a dark "tag" hangs below the front arch). Fix the liners to dark and remove the stray fin/mud-flap geometry, or make it a proper mud flap.
4. **Front face has a dated, "chopped" look.** The twin round lamps inside rectangular black housings read well as an 80s hatch, but the housings are huge, the grille is a small black slab squeezed between them, the number plate sits under the grille like a sticker, and the bumper is a plain thick black bar. Shrink the lamp housings 15%, enlarge the grille and give it a body-coloured surround, and shape the bumper (an end return, a lip). The bonnet again has a fine shut line but no relief: add a slight raised centre or side creases.
5. **Body side is bland.** A single black rubbing strip and a rectangular door outline are the only features. The lower door line forms a rectangle that looks like a hatch panel, not a door. Add a shoulder crease or beltline highlight, a shaped sill, and a small flare over each wheel arch. A visible C-pillar plus a Golf-style thick pillar with a black panel would give identity.
6. **Rear.** The tail lamps are rounded rectangles with a hard black edge and white reversing lamps below; they are large and read well, but the black bumper is a blocky shelf. The tailgate has no handle, no badge area, no wiper, and the rear window is a huge dark slab. Add a rear wiper and a tailgate shut line and shape the bumper.
7. **Glass.** Same as the banger: flat dark grey with a black surround, interior barely visible. The small front quarter window is a nice period detail but the two glass shapes do not agree with the door line.
8. **Small details.** Mirrors are the same tiny stubs on stalks; a larger, flatter mirror with a visible housing would help. Add fuel flap, antenna, and a roof rail option for variety.

Class read: it reads like a plain family car (good) but it looks heavier than the banger, not "cheap/small". It also looks more like a boxy SUV, so it clashes with the intended "Golf/Panda" idea. Class C should read as small and light: shorten the length by about 8%, lower the roof, and keep the wheel size large relative to the body.

## 3. test_wedge (class A 1970s wedge supercar) — 5/10

Current read: the best of the three. It reads as a fast mid-engine car at once (long cabin set back, wide rear haunches, a big wing, a ribbed side scoop, pop-up lamps, 5-spoke wheels). But the shape is a bulbous "C5 Corvette blob" rather than a 70s wedge.

Improvements, most impactful first:

1. **Not a wedge: nose is tall and bulbous.** The side view shows a high rounded nose, a bonnet that bulges upward in the middle (the pop-up lamp pods and a raised cowl), and a front splitter that only reaches a small distance. A 70s wedge (Countach/Stratos/Esprit) has a low pointed nose, a straight or slightly concave bonnet line rising in one straight line to the base of the windscreen, and a rear that rises to the roof. Lower the nose 15-20%, sharpen the front edge into a visible leading edge with a crease, and run the bonnet and the windscreen as one continuous slope. Remove the bulge in front of the windscreen.
2. **Front fender flares are ragged and look glued on.** In the 34front and nose views the black flare around the front wheel has a jagged, uneven outline with a small black triangular "shard" under it, and the flare sits proud of the body with a visible gap and no blend. The rear flare looks similar (ragged edge in the 34rear). Make the flares smooth, continuous extrusions of the body, with an even lip thickness, and make the top of the front arch lower than the bonnet line so the wheel does not poke through the body mass.
3. **Pop-up lamp lids look like glitches.** In the 34front and top views the lids are rounded-rectangle outlines with a black thin vane or fin sticking out from one side (like a broken flap). They do not read as lamps up or down. Make the lids flush panels with a clear hinge line; add a second state or a clear lamp glass; and remove the black vane, or turn it into a proper hinge. The result reads as scratches in the bonnet.
4. **Rear: wing and tail.** The wing is far too big and too high for a 70s wedge, floats on thin black posts, and in the side view is a black block (the end plate) with a gap to the body, so it reads as a box stuck to the tail. Lower it, shorten the span a little, round the end plates, and mount it on the tail deck. The tail face is a blunt vertical slab with a wide slatted black grille, two long red lamps with small white reversing lamps beside them, and the tailpipes poking out low beneath the bumper. The rear overhang is very short and the tail is truncated; add a rear taper, a lip spoiler, a diffuser, and move the exhaust into a recess. The rear deck looks flat and empty between the cabin and the wing: add an engine cover with louvres or a visible engine bay, which would also sell "mid-engine".
5. **Wheels and stance.** The 5-spoke wheels are good (the best of the three), but the tyres are tall for a supercar and the same width front and rear. A supercar should have larger rims with thinner sidewalls and a rear tyre about 10-15% wider than the front. Lower the ride height a touch more and tuck the splitter closer to the ground. The ortho side view again shows the white crescent over each wheel (the arch rendering fault).
6. **Paint and material.** The body is a cream-mustard that looks dull; the highlight on the bonnet is nice but the colour does not say "premium". Choose a more saturated colour (a hot orange, a rich yellow, a deep metallic), add a clearcoat with a strong highlight line down the shoulders, and consider a stripe or a livery. The black parts (splitter, sills, wing posts) are all the same matte black; give the splitter a thinner edge (it looks chewed and thick at the nose), and add some chrome/aluminium accents.
7. **Cabin and glass.** The cabin shape is good (a wedge-shaped glasshouse with a long rake and a rear quarter scoop). The windscreen has a thick black base that reads as a heavy dashboard cowl; thin it. The glass has some interior visible (steering wheel and seats), which is the best glass of the three. Add a visible pillar and a thin chrome glass edge, and make the rear window deeper and more raked. The side window outline has an odd bump at the front corner; smooth it.
8. **Details.** The number plate on the nose is a blurry white blob (looks like a texture error); replace with a clean rectangle or remove it for the race. The orange indicator at the front is large and long; shorten it. The side intake with ribs is good; add a door handle, a fuel filler, and a small vent behind the front wheel.

Class read: it does look fast and expensive next to the other two, and the wing, lamps, wheels and low stance make the tier jump obvious. What undermines it is the bulbous nose and the oversized wing, which read as "tuner car" or "Corvette-ish" rather than a refined wedge, and the dull paint.

## Tier difference

The class gap is visible mostly through the wedge's wing, flares and wheels. Banger and hatch are both C and look like siblings: similar tall tyres, pale or flat paint, the same mirror, glass and lamp treatment, and the same hairline door lines. Between C and A, the missing steps are:

- stance (width, ride height, tyre profile),
- surface complexity (the wedge has flares and scoops, the C cars are plain),
- material (all paint is the same flat gloss; no metallic, no trim variation),
- detail count (the wedge has pop-ups and grilles; the C cars have little).

The intermediate classes (B) will have nothing to bridge into unless the generator exposes these as tier-driven parameters.

---

## Top 5 generator-level changes (help every car)

1. **Body-to-cabin blending and surfacing.** Add shoulder/beltline fillets, tumblehome, and panel relief (bonnet creases, raised centre, shaped sills, rolled arch lips) so the body is no longer smoothed boxes with a box greenhouse. Right now every car is "rounded slab plus separate glasshouse".
2. **Wheel-arch system.** One shared arch builder with a dark liner, a clean even lip, optional flares that are continuous with the body, and no stray fin or shard geometry. Fix the white crescent artifact above every wheel in the side view. Tie arch gap and flare size to tier.
3. **Wheel and tyre library with tier-driven stance.** More rim designs (steel dish, hubcap, multi-spoke, mesh, aero) with proper hubs and lip, tyre profile and chamfer by class, front/rear width stagger for upper tiers, and ride height and track width as class parameters. Today every car has tall, similar tyres.
4. **Paint, glass and interior pass.** A palette with real saturation and clearcoat highlights, optional two-tone/stripe/wear layers (banger wear, premium gloss/metallic), lighter glass tint with a simple visible interior (dash, seats, wheel), and window surrounds with thickness. The paints currently look washed out against the grey environment, and the glass is a flat dark slab.
5. **Face and detail kit scaled to the face.** Size lamps, grille, plate and bumper as a fraction of the face width, with proper bumper profiles per era (chrome blade, rubber, integrated splitter), consistent plate recesses (the blurry plate on the wedge and the bumpy plate on the banger are faults), larger mirrors with brackets, handles, wipers, exhausts, fuel caps, and a rear wiper for hatches. Also add per-archetype silhouette rules (wedge: low pointed nose and continuous slope; hatch: raked tailgate and short roof) so cars are not variations of the same three-box.
