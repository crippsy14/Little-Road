# Quarry Hills street review, round 2 (qh2)

Photos: review\quarry-hills\streets-qh2\streets\quarry-hills\. All left and right photos of the seven streets were reviewed: high-street 12 locations, quarry-road 13, quay-lane 15, haul-road 11, chapel-street 10, terrace-row 7, kettle-falls-road 2 (70 locations, 140 photos). "Along" photos were opened only for quay-lane/014. Not reported, as instructed: the thin slate-slab front fences (crawiau).

Note on method: several of my reads returned no image mid-session. The chapel-street, terrace-row, haul-road 004R-010R, quay-lane 014 and high-street 000 findings were re-checked against the rendered photos. The rest is from the first pass plus a second pass in which the images rendered. Coordinates are approximate (1920x1080) and where I could not re-confirm a detail I say "possible".

## Terrace rows: overall verdict
The new terrace rows hold together well. I found no gaps between neighbours, no roof clashes, no chimney standing inside a neighbour, no house floating or sunk (apart from QH2-01), and no house blocking a road or a drive. Rooflines, party walls, dormers and hip/gable joins meet cleanly in every row I looked at, including the awkward corners (terrace-row 000R, 004R, high-street 004/005, chapel-street 000R). The recurring problems are signs hidden under balconies, lamps and parked cars standing in front of doors, and blank or blanked-out walls.

## Not seen
Nothing was unreadable in the end. No z-fighting, no walls across doorways or drives, and no road-side drops without a bank were seen. The quarry-floor edge on haul-road 009/010 slopes away gently rather than dropping, so I did not list it.

## Recommendations (8)
1. Fix the shopfront sign placement once for all shops: move signs below the balcony floor (or drop the balcony floor level), and keep lamps at least one house-width from a fascia. That clears QH2-02 and QH2-03 together.
2. Space the lamps along the pavement against the plot boundaries so none stands in front of a door or window; the same for benches and bins.
3. Stop spawning parked cars on front lawns. Give each shop a small kerb-side parking bay or keep cars on the road, and use the lawns for planters, bins and bikes.
4. Replace the stone-fill "bricked" upper windows with real window glass, or add a deliberate texture (a painted fake window or a shuttered window); most 3-storey houses use it.
5. Give blank gables something to look at: a faded mural (a slate-quarry or rugby scene), a painted shop advert, a chapel plaque, a drainpipe and a couple of small windows, or a lean-to.
6. Tidy the village square: make the paving fill the plot cleanly (no grass bites), turn the bus shelter into a solid prop (it is a dither ghost), give the flags a folded cloth and set the camera clear of them, and use darker lettering on the INSTITUTE sign.
7. Finish the edges: continue the pavement round the corners (or end it with a proper kerb return), and replace the sandy-dirt strips at junctions with kerbed pavement and verge; cap lone gateposts with a wall or remove them.
8. Dress the empty meadows (quarry-road 000-001, kettle-falls, quay-lane 011-012, haul-road 007-008) with the town's own props: dry-stone walls that join up, sheep, a stile and footpath sign, a slate-spoil heap, a few farm buildings, and replace the floating boulders and lone wall stubs.
