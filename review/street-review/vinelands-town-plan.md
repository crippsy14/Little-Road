# Cumeada de São Leonardo: a Douro-style wine town (Vinelands plan, rev. 2)

Local frame: a = metres east, n = metres north, origin = the Largo (150 m). This revision follows the owner's brief change: nothing from the earlier road/building list is fixed. Kept from the site: the N-S ridge, the east-facing terraced amphitheatre, the river (about 1 km east), the far bank, the gentle Vale shelf to the west, a ~1.8 km district, the entry from the neighbouring village at (-620, 1240), and a road along the river. Everything else is re-planned. Where an old feature was in the right place for a real reason (Largo, Calçada, Cais, three quintas, bell tower on the knoll), it stays; where it was not (the through road, the co-op, the circuit), it moves.

The town is called **Cumeada** ("the crest"); the district and its entry road are "Vinelands". About 3,400 people live in the freguesia (3,000 in the built-up areas, 400 scattered).

## Principles (why it is shaped like this)
1. **Towns sit on the dry crest and farm the slopes.** The Largo is on the ridge shoulder at 150 m, on granite, with a spring on the west side (Fonte Velha). Vines go on the schist of the east slope, where the sun is, and below the town so grapes go downhill.
2. **The through road must not use the Largo.** The only low crossing of the ridge is the saddle at (-160, 700), 108 m. The neighbouring-village road crosses there and keeps descending to the river. The town hangs off it by a 700 m spur (Rua Direita), as many hill towns hang off a national road.
3. **Wine flows downhill.** The co-operative is at the road junction near the river, not over the ridge. The Calçada links town to quay, and a mid-slope tractor road ties the estates together.
4. **Noise and spectacle are separated from the Largo.** Racing is on the Vale shelf, behind the ridge, with the town as grandstand. The hill climb uses the empty south bowl and finishes at the Belvedere.

## 1. Town structure and growth story
**Phase 0 (Roman to 12th c.):** a ridge track (Estrada Velha, now Rua Direita and the Mirante road) with a spring on the west side and a chapel where a cattle route crossed it, at the Largo.
**12th-15th c.:** Vila Velha. Houses crowd round the church on the east shoulder, sheltered from the Atlantic wind, facing the morning sun, in a fan of stepped lanes falling down the slope. Granite, tight, defensive.
**1500s:** a Franciscan convent on the north-east edge (cerca walls, now the Jardim da Cerca). After 1834 it became a garden and the cloister is a ruin.
**18th c. wine boom:** terraces are cut, three quintas are founded, the Cais and its lodges are built, and the Calçada (the road) and the Calçada Velha (the mule steps) link Largo and quay. Merchants build the 18th/19th-c. houses along Rua Direita, which becomes the carriage road north to the saddle.
**After phylloxera (1880s):** terraces rebuilt, a railway on the far bank (1887), a ferry at the Cais. Brazil returnees build tile-fronted villas along the Mirante road.
**1950s-60s:** the co-op Adega (1956) at the river-road junction, with co-op housing; Ponte Nova (1957) replaces the ferry; the market hall and school; the emigrant years (France) add small detached houses.
**1980s-now:** the Alto da Portela estate on the west flank, school, pitch, health centre and fire station on the saddle; bulldozed wide terraces on the better quinta land; the 1.6 km Vale club circuit; wine tourism at the lodges.

Result: an old core on the crest, ribbons running north and south along the ridge, and a thin line of river hamlet and cooperative below. The town grew along the ridge, not down the slope: the slope is farmed.

## 5. The countryside
Geology drives it: granite at the crest (olives, rye, the town), schist on the amphitheatre (vines), mixed sandy soils on the Vale shelf (farmland, orchards), poor stony ground in the south and west (cork oak and scrub).

### Amphitheatre (east slope)
Terraces are stone-walled benches every 5 m of height (about 36 m apart on average, narrower and steeper near the top). Three ownership layers:
1. **Courelas de Cima, smallholder belt**, polygon (60,-330) (290,-330) (300,650) (110,650). Hundreds of 0.1-0.3 ha plots owned by town families: mixed vines, a few olives on the edges, vegetables, figs, a stone hut and a water tank per plot, bicycles and mopeds on the paths. Near town because people tend them in the evening.
2. **Mid-slope quinta land** between a=300 and the Estrada do Meio, and the lower slope to the river road.
3. **Mortórios** (abandoned terraces) at the steep tops and corners where heirs disagree: grey with broom, rock-rose and wild olive; walls collapsing.

## 6. The river
**West bank:** the river road, with the Cais and its quay wall; north of it, the Passeio Ribeirinho and the beach; south, a plain road along the bank, olives and the lower quinta land. **Cais:** a stone quay 120 m long at (985,0), three lodges behind it, the slipway and a floating pontoon for two or three traditional wine boats (barcos) that carry tourists now. **Bridge:** Ponte Nova at (995,-70) to (1110,-70), 115 m, three concrete arches. It is at the Cais because the rock bars narrow the river and the old ferry crossed there. 7.5 t limit. **Railway:** single track, east bank, on a shelf 12 m above the water, two short tunnels where spurs reach the water, around n=+900 and n=-850. Halt "Cumeada-Cais" at (1135,-60), a platform 60 m long with a 12x6 m building and a goods shed; four trains a day each way.

### 6a. Hill climb: Subida das Seis Voltas
A 2.26 km road (average about 9.7%, lower legs 6-8%, upper legs 10-12%, finishing ramp ~13%) built in the 1930s as the "estrada do Mirante" to link the river to the knoll. **Start** at the river road at (1115,-1000), with a paddock on the riverside flat (Veiga do Fojo) at (1040,-1010), 150x80 m (kale and maize in normal years). Hairpins at (915,-1130), (715,-930), (515,-1130), (315,-930), (115,-1130), (-85,-930), legs about 280 m each, 8 m wide with flattened apexes. Finish ramp (-85,-930) (-250,-860) (-400,-790) (-520,-700) (-590,-614) to a car park at the Belvedere knoll (230 m). Why here: the south bowl is the biggest unbroken stretch of slope, it needs no houses, it has the best view of the whole amphitheatre, and the knoll is a natural finish. Spectators stand on the terrace walls at the hairpins (hay bales along olive lines); the return is by the Mirante road to the Largo and the Calçada to the Cais, so the whole event forms a loop.

### 6b. Circuito da Cumeada (club road circuit)
1.6 km, 7 m wide, built from farm lanes widened and closed on race days, **counter-clockwise on the Vale shelf** behind the ridge: (-470,-260) start/finish, 320 m straight north (-470,60), (-490,150), (-550,225) right-hand sweep, west along the north side (-680,250) (-800,225), (-880,150) fast left, (-905,50), (-880,-60), (-820,-140), (-770,-200), a right-left chicane (-710,-170) (-650,-215), (-570,-255) and back. It falls 18 m to the west and climbs back along the south side. The paddock and clubhouse are in the infield at (-540,-60), 120x70 m, entered from the Caminho Velho do Vale. The Eiras balconies and the flank terraces above (-400,0) look down on the straight, 100-250 m away, so no stands are needed. It runs around the town, not through it; the farm lanes are normal farm roads between meetings.

## 7. Daily life and rhythms
- **Dawn (6:30):** bread at the oven in the Pátio do Forno; bread vans leave for outlying farms. Old men at the Café Central.
- **Morning (7:30-9):** school buses at the Portela and the terminus; parents leave cars on the Terreiro; workers walk the Calçada Velha to the quay. Delivery vans reach the Largo before 9:30 via Rua Direita and unload at its north side. Lorries are barred from Vila Velha and use the Estrada do Meio and the river road.
- **Midday:** workers' lunch at the Cais tascas and the Adega canteen; church bells and the siren at the fire station at noon; Jardim da Cerca benches.
- **Afternoon:** Largo quiet; pastelaria busy; kids from school at the Portela courts.
- **Evening (18:00-21:00):** Passeio Ribeirinho stroll, football training, the Largo terraces, café TV for the match.
- **Market:** Wednesday (small) and the second Sunday (fair) on the Terreiro; fish vans Tuesday and Thursday on the Largo.
- **Harvest (September):** tractors with grape trailers come down the tracks from the terraces to the Estrada do Meio, then north to the Adega (1.8 km, mostly flat), queue at the weighbridge on the Estrada de Vinelands from dawn, and turn round on the river road. Smallholders bring grapes in pickups and on mopeds. Quinta grapes go to their own lagar by the yard. Some lorries carry boxes down the Calçada to the lodges. In the evenings you smell must at the Cais.
- **Tourists:** coaches use the Terreiro; they walk to the Largo, the Jardim, the Belvedere by minibus, and the Cais for boat trips; the Calçada Velha is a hike. Race and climb weekends bring extra cars to the Vale lanes and the Veiga.
- **Parking:** Terreiro (80), Portela school lot (60), supermarket (25), Cais strip (40), Adega lot (50). Vans park in the bays on the north side of the Largo and at the Cais.

## 8. Landmarks and views
**Landmarks:** (1) the Largo and church tower; (2) the Belvedere bell tower on the 230 m knoll; (3) the Cais with its three lodges and wine boats; (4) the Seis Voltas hairpins stacked on the south bowl; (5) the terraced amphitheatre itself, a great staircase of walls; (6) Ponte Nova and the halt; (7) the Moinhos do Alto, three ruined windmills; (8) the Pombal dovecote and cypress avenue; (9) the Adega's long arched façade and chimney; (10) the Jardim da Cerca ruined cloister arch.

**Views:** (1) from the church terrace and the Jardim wall (190,100): dawn over the amphitheatre to the river; (2) from the Belvedere (-590,-614): 360 degrees, the Vale and circuit on the west, the terraces to the river on the east; (3) from the third bend of the Calçada (700,-60): looking back at the town skyline on the crest; (4) from Ponte Nova (1050,-70): the whole amphitheatre rising to the town and the quay; (5) from the Portela lay-by (-160,700): river on the left, Vale on the right. Bonus: from the railway halt at sunset the terraces glow.

## 10. Phasing (5 stages, most identity first)
1. **The crest town and its link to the river.** Largo, church, Vila Velha lanes and stairs, Rua Direita façades, the Calçada and Calçada Velha, the Cais with the three lodges and quay. This is the identity: white town on a ridge above a staircase of vines.
2. **The working slope.** Terrace parcels, walls, mortórios, huts, farm tracks, the three quintas and their yards, the Estrada do Meio, the Casal do Meio hamlet. Add the riverside strip, Ponte Nova, the halt and the first far-bank terraces.
3. **Hill climb and the Belvedere.** The Seis Voltas road, the Veiga paddock, the Mirante road with villas, the bell-tower knoll, the Moinhos do Alto and the cemetery.
4. **Daily-life layer and the north.** Market, terminus, Jardim da Cerca, Portela (school, pitch, health centre, fire station, flats), the Entroncamento (Adega, bottling, cooper, workshops), the entry road and the harvest queue.
5. **The Vale.** Club circuit with paddock, hortas, almond and olive groves, montado, farmsteads and eiras, the cork depot, the far-bank hamlet and ruin, plus the seasonal dressing (harvest trailers, drying almonds, market stalls).
