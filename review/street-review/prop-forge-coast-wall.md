# Prop Forge coast wall: Bayview road review (pf-streets-1)

Reviewed 17 stations (000-016), along and right views, plus 000-right and 002-along. Left views not opened (not needed for the right-hand wall).

## Verdict

The new dry-stone wall mostly works. It reads as a convincing grey wall with a lumpy cope. It sits on the ground along its whole length with no floating or buried runs, no cracks between segments, and it never crosses the road. It stops cleanly at the Hillcrest junction (003-right) with a pier at each end. The main problem is that the old pale cream ashlar wall still appears in the 530-610 m stretch and butts straight up against the new wall, so the coast wall changes style twice within about 60 m. No high-severity defects.

## Wall findings

1. 013-along, 014-along, 014-right, 015-right (medium, high confidence): Between the rock outcrops at about 530-610 m, the right-hand wall is the old pale cream ashlar (flat cope, clean rectangular blocks). It sits next to the new grey dry-stone wall. In 014-along (right of centre) the new wall's pier stands about 1 m from the start of the pale wall, so the two styles meet with a visible gap. In 015-right the pale wall begins at the right edge, beside the big rock, after a 5 m grass gap with no pier. The coast wall changes style twice in about 60 m.
2. 015-right (low, medium confidence): The new wall ends in a pier, then there is open grass before the pale wall. The gap reads as intended, but the pale wall has no pier and ends square.
3. 009-right (low, low confidence): At the hairpin, centre of the frame, the wall climbs the bank steeply around the bend. The cope and course lines look tilted or stretched there, though the wall is not floating.
4. 005-right, 008-right, 010-right, 011-right, 012-right, 016-right (low, medium confidence): The piers are flat-capped boxes with a small raised block on top. They read a bit like bins or letterboxes rather than stone piers. They are consistent but plain.
5. 003-right (low, medium confidence): A yield sign stands touching the pier at the left end of the junction gap, with its pole hard against the pier face. It looks tight but does not clearly intersect.
6. 009-right, 010-right, 011-along (low, medium confidence): Chevron signs stand right behind the wall or pier, with their feet hidden behind the cope. They read as placed too close to the wall rather than clipping it.
7. 002-along (low, low confidence): At the right edge, a palm trunk rises directly behind the large near pier. The trunk and pier may overlap.

## Not the wall

8. 008-along, top left (medium, medium confidence): A building sits on the hillcrest with its stone basement face exposed down the slope. It looks cut off or hovering at the slope edge.
9. 007-along (top right) and 008-right (top right) (low, medium confidence): A rock has a hard flat square cut at its base, as if clipped by a plane.
10. 013-right (near the rock base) and 013-along (right rock face) (low, high confidence): A small pink and blue sign card sits flat on the rock face, with no post.
11. 002-right, 003-right, 005-right, 014-right (low, medium confidence): Distant cliffs and headlands show stretched vertical streak texture.
12. 013-right (low, medium confidence): Loose angular boulders sit scattered at the rock base. They look dropped in, with sharp unweathered slab faces.
